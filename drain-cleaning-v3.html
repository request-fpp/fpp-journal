H2: The Washing Machine Standpipe and the Dishwasher Line

The washer drains into a standpipe in the wall, and the trap at the bottom of it collects what the washer sends: lint, hair, threads, the dirt out of the clothes. The dishwasher is a different story, and it's about how it was connected. The right way is a drain hose to an air gap on the countertop, and from the air gap to the garbage disposal. H2: Newer Houses and Older Houses: PVC, Slope and Cast Iron

A newer house with PVC drain lines is the easy case. The pipe is smooth, the fall the plumber set is still there, and there isn't much on the walls yet. Those lines clog because of what went down them, and they clear and stay clear. As the house ages, even PVC changes. The ground under the slab moves, the foundation settles, an inch here and two there, nothing you see in the walls, and a drain line that was set with a fall toward the main loses it. Water that used to run now crawls, and what's in it settles and builds. That's how a line in a house with no visible problem starts clogging again and again.

Cast iron is the hard case. In the Plano houses from the 70s and 80s the drains under the floor are cast iron, 30 to 50 years old, rusted and scaled inside, pinched where it shifted, cracked in places. Every rough spot catches what goes by. Toilet paper is made to fall apart in water. Wet wipes are not, and in a cast iron line the rust and the sharp turns catch them and start the next stoppage.
