H2: The Plumbing Calls We Get Most in Frisco

These are the plumbing services Frisco homeowners call us for most. Each one has its own page.

A sink that won't drain, with the AC line tied in

This is the top call right now, here like everywhere else we work. The AC condensate line is tied into the sink drain under the cabinet. Soap, hair, grease, calcium and dirt build up in the sink line while the AC keeps dripping water into it all day. Then it clogs, the sink fills, and water runs into the cabinet. If the faucet is closed and the cabinet is wet, look for the small pipe near the trap. In the photo it is the black line, marked, coming into the drain above the trap, and the cable is in that drain line clearing it. We clear the line, and you hear what to expect with it before we start. Drain cleaning has the details.

The pressure reducing valve (PRV): too high or too low

The PRV sits in a box at the front of the house, between the meter and the house. We replace them all the time, for three reasons: hard water scales them up, they wear out with age, and the builder's box is often full of dirt, so nobody sees the valve until it fails. When a PRV fails high, pressure climbs over 80 PSI and starts eating cartridges, fill valves and the water heater. When it fails low, the shower fades. A gauge on the hose bib tells us in a minute. And the third way: the valve itself starts to leak in its box. The clip here is a PRV leaking in its box, water running out of the old valve into the mud. We dug it out and put in a new valve with a new shut-off valve next to it. PRV replacement has the details.

Water heaters, garage or attic, and the expansion tank

Another call we get all the time. Most of them fail from age and from never being touched: nobody flushes the tank, the anode rod is never changed, the expansion tank is never checked. We say this without blame, because nobody tells a homeowner to do it. The expansion tank is the quiet one. There is a rubber bladder inside, it cracks with time, water fills the tank, the tank rusts, and one day it drips onto the heater. A scaled tank also drops your hot water pressure. Water heater repair and replacement and expansion tank replacement are separate pages, because they are separate jobs.

Outside spigots that crack or won't shut off

The spigot on your wall is a frost free one. If the builder and the plumber installed it right, it should not freeze or burst. It still needs a cover before a freeze, and that is what our reminders say: let the faucets inside the house drip, put covers on the outside spigots, find the main shut-off valve before you need it. It helps. And still, every spring and half the summer we replace outdoor spigots that cracked inside the wall during the freeze. The crack shows nothing until the first time you turn the hose on, and then water runs into the wall. Others simply leak: you close the handle and the water keeps running. Nothing burst, it just will not shut off. Hose bib repair has the details, and the guide on the main shut-off valve shows how to stop the water fast if a pipe does burst.

Main sewer lines with roots, and backups that come back

The soil here moves. Joints in the sewer line open a little, roots find the water, and a year later the toilets back up. We clear the roots with the chain snake and run a camera through the line. Sometimes the camera goes in first, when the line is clogged but still lets it through. Sometimes it goes in after the clearing, because in a packed line the camera shows nothing. It depends on how bad the clog is. If the camera shows a separated joint, we repair that section. Sewer line repair and camera inspection has the details. A backup that comes back after clearing is the sign.

Dripping faucets, cartridges and tub spouts

Hard water, same as the rest of Texas. Calcium builds up inside the cartridge and breaks down the rubber, and the faucet drips or won't shut off. One we pulled came out crusted white, it is in the photo here. The tub spout goes the same way. The diverter inside it, the part that sends the water up to the shower head when you pull the knob, scales up, its rubber and plastic wear through, and then water runs from the spout and the shower head at the same time. Faucet and shower valve repair has the details.

Garbage disposals that jam or leak

In the newer homes the builder puts in the most basic unit there is: a third or half a horsepower, with a one year warranty from the manufacturer. It handles heavy food scraps badly, it jams and clogs, and in two or three years it stops or starts to leak from the bottom. A disposal like that is not worth repairing, it gets replaced. We put in Moen 3/4 horsepower units with a ten year manufacturer warranty. Garbage disposal repair has the details.

H2: Water Pressure in Frisco Homes: What the Gauge Shows

We put a gauge on the hose bib at a lot of houses here. Very often it reads over 80 PSI, and 90 is not rare. In a few homes we have seen 105. Above 80 the whole system is under stress, so when we put in a new PRV, we usually set it to 70 PSI. Then there is the other end. With hard water an old PRV rusts and clogs inside until almost nothing gets through, and the gauge shows 30 PSI. Too high or too low, it is usually the same valve. Check your pressure once a year.

In the newer homes we keep finding the same hidden leak, and it was made when the house was built: a shelf nail driven through a water line behind the drywall, most often PEX, that plugs its own hole for a year or two and then rusts through. H2: Backflow Preventers on Sprinkler Systems

We replace backflow preventers on sprinkler systems often. Most of them simply rust. The handles and the shut-off valves on the backflow rust until they will not turn, and then you cannot close the water. After the backflow the line is usually PVC, and in the ground that plastic is brittle: the ground moves or a root presses on it, and the pipe cracks.
