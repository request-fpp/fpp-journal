The Call We Get Five Times a Day Right Now

A sink that will not drain, and then water running out of the cabinet. In a lot of houses here the AC condensate line is tied into the drain under a sink, and that is the line that clogs. The sink side brings soap, hair, dirt, everything people rinse off. The AC side brings its own: bacteria, rust, calcium. Together they close the pipe.

Once it is closed, the AC keeps sending water into it. An AC unit can push close to 20 gallons a day down that line, so a clogged one fills the sink, goes over the edge and soaks the cabinet floor, sometimes the floor under it. If you see water under a sink and the faucet is off, look where the small AC drain pipe comes into the cabinet.

We clear it, show you what came out, and tell you straight: this line clogs again. Nobody can stop the AC from sending gunk into it. What we can do is clear it properly so it runs, and you know what you are looking at the next time. Drain cleaning has the details.

What Our Warranty Covers, and What No Plumber Can Promise

Our work carries a warranty: generally six months to a year, a year on water heaters and a year on slab leak repairs. If something we installed or repaired fails because of our work, we come back and fix it.

The warranty covers what is under our control. Some things are not, and we would rather say so now than argue later.

A kitchen line in a house where a lot of cooking happens is one. We have a customer whose line we clear about every six months. The camera shows the pipe in perfect shape, nothing cracked, nothing sagging, and it still fills with grease. They even get a drain cleaner delivered every month and use it every month. That is not a broken pipe. That is how the line is used, and no warranty from anybody covers it.

Toilets are another. Out of toilets we have pulled pens, toothbrushes, toy cars, golf balls, detergent caps, and once a whole towel. Out of tubs, enough hair to make a wig.

Hard water is the third. We replace a shower cartridge, and a year later it fails again, because the water here carries a lot of calcium and it eats the rubber in the cartridge. That is not our work failing and it is not your fault either. Whoever installed that cartridge, the same water would wear it out.

If something comes back, the plumber shows you what caused it before anything is charged.
