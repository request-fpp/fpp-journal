How a Call Works

It's the same day or night. [Each step with a real photo.]

  1. You call or text. We'll ask what you see, what you hear, and if the water is still running. If it is, we tell you what to close before we get there.
  2. You get an arrival window. It depends on where the closest plumber is at that moment. If we have the time open, you don't wait. If it's a real emergency, we move our own schedule around for you.
  3. The plumber finds the cause. Meter, gauge, camera, acoustic equipment, whatever the problem needs. On a weekday that's the $49 service call, and the $49 goes toward the repair.
  4. You hear the price before we open anything. It's a flat price for the job. If there are two or three ways to fix it, each one has its own price, and you pick.
  5. We fix it once and we fix it right. Copper under a slab gets brazed and pressure tested before we fill the hole. Your valve gets the right cartridge. A cleanout goes where the code wants it.
  6. You get an invoice that says what we did. On bigger jobs we take photos, and our plumbers wear camera glasses, so even the part under the slab or inside the wall is on video. Just ask and we'll send it.

What Our Warranty Covers, and What No Plumber Can Promise

Our labor has a basic six month warranty. Water heaters and slab leak repairs have one year. If something fails because of our work, we come back and fix it.

But I can't give you a warranty on things that aren't under my control, and I'd rather tell you that now than have you frustrated later.

Drains are the big one. After we unclog a line we see what was in it: grease, dirt, food. If oil and greasy food keep going down the kitchen sink, or heavy food goes into the garbage disposal, I can tell you the truth, that line is going to clog again.

Toilets, same thing. Wet wipes are not supposed to be flushed. Say it's an older house, almost 50 years old, with cast iron pipe. That pipe already has rust buildup inside and a lot of sharp turns, and the rust, the calcium, the grease and those turns catch the wipes and cause the next stoppage. Flush only regular toilet paper. And you'd be surprised what we pull out of toilets: toy cars, toothbrushes, combs. Right after the house cleaners leave we get a lot of clogged toilets with cleaning stuff in them. Out of one main drain line we pulled a metal thermos, metal mugs and a small garden shovel.

Slab leaks happen because the foundation shifts and the ground shifts and puts extra pressure on the pipe, or tree roots get to it. Our repair has a year. A new leak somewhere else under the house is not something I can control.

Hard water eats rubber. Shower, tub and faucet cartridges have rubber inside, calcium builds up in them, and that's the main reason they fail. It eats the plastic too, and in a toilet it eats the flapper and the rubber in the fill valve. And please don't put those cleaning tablets in the tank, the ones that turn the water blue. They eat all the rubber in there. Never do that.

High pressure is the last one. A lot of houses here have water pressure over 80 PSI, and then the whole system is under stress: your pipes, your fittings, your plastic connections, your rubber gaskets. Measure your water pressure once a year. That's how you avoid a flood, a broken cartridge or a broken faucet.
