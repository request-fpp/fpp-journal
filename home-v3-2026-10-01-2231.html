What Our Warranty Covers, and What No Plumber Can Promise

We give a basic six month warranty on our labor, and one year on water heaters and on slab leak repairs. But we can't give any warranty on a job that is not under my control. With all respect, I can't control what someone flushes down the toilet. Do you flush wet wipes? Do you pour oil or greasy stuff down your kitchen sink? Stuff like that is not under my control, and that's why I can't give you any warranty on it.

Because after we unclogged your drain line, we were able to see a lot of greasy stuff, a lot of dirt. I don't want you to be frustrated next time, because I can tell you the truth: you definitely will have this issue again. It's not under my control. If you put greasy stuff in your kitchen sink, like oil and greasy food, or if you put some heavy food stuff inside your garbage disposal, you will definitely have this issue again. So I just don't want you to be frustrated. That's why I'm telling you up front.

Or say it's a pretty old house. It was built almost 50 years ago, and you have cast iron pipes. And of course, it matters when you flush wet wipes down the toilet, which we don't recommend. Wet wipes are not supposed to be flushed. Your old cast iron pipe already has a lot of rust buildup inside and a lot of sharp turns, and every time you flush wet wipes, the rust, the calcium, the grease and the sharp turns can catch the wipes and cause the next stoppage. That's why you need to use your toilet pretty carefully. Flush only regular toilet paper to avoid the same thing in the near future.

What we pull out of toilets: toy cars, toys, toothbrushes, combs. Right after the house cleaners leave we get a lot of clogged toilets, and it's cleaning stuff in there. Out of one main drain line we pulled a metal thermos, metal mugs, even a small garden shovel.

Slab leaks happen because the foundation shifted, the ground shifted, and some extra pressure was created on the pipe. Or maybe some tree roots damaged the pipe. So it's not under my control.

Hard water is a big one here. Shower, tub and faucet cartridges have rubber inside, and hard water breaks that rubber down. Calcium builds up inside the cartridge, and that's the main reason they fail. It eats plastic too. In a toilet it eats the flapper and the rubber in the fill valve. And please don't put those cleaning tablets in the toilet tank, the ones that turn the water blue. They eat all the rubber in the tank. Never do that.

A lot of houses here have water pressure over 80 PSI, and then the whole system is under stress. And this extra pressure could also damage your water system, your pipes, your fittings, your plastic connections, your rubber gaskets and all this stuff, because the system is under stress when you have pretty high water pressure. That's why you need to check and measure your water pressure every year, to avoid any flooding, any broken cartridge, any broken faucet.
