Water pressure in McKinney is not one number. On one street the gauge on the hose bib reads 108 PSI, and across town a house gets 58 PSI from the main and never more. In most houses here it sits in a plastic box in the flower bed by the foundation, with the second shut-off valve next to it, and both get buried a little deeper every year the mulch is topped up. When somebody finally digs one up, it is clogged, stuck or leaking at the stem, and often all three at once.

Slab leaks, and the ground under the house

The soil here is clay, and it moves. After a dry summer it shrinks, after a wet winter it swells, the foundation shifts a little, and the copper under the slab shifts with it. When that system has quit and nobody noticed, the ground moves more, and the leaks follow, in the water lines and in the drain lines under the floor. A warm spot on the floor, a water bill that jumped, the sound of water with everything off: that is the call.
