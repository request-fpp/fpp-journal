Title: PRV / Water Pressure Regulator Replacement | Frisco & Plano

Meta description: City water comes in at 100 PSI and more; the PRV holds it at 70. Pressure test, PRV and shut-off valve replaced with the city permit, in Frisco and Plano.

H1: Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV) Replacement for Low or High Water Pressure in Frisco & Plano

The city sends water to your house at whatever pressure it takes to reach every house on the line: 80 PSI and up, 100 in some places, 120, sometimes 140. The pressure reducing valve, the PRV, is the one part between the meter and your faucets that brings that down to what a house is built for. When it fails, you get one of three things: the water goes weak, the water goes wild, or the box in the flower bed fills with water. All three end with a new valve, and almost always a new shut-off valve beside it.

FPP Plumbing replaces PRVs from the Frisco office and the Plano office, with our own licensed plumbers, two on every shift. We put a gauge on the house first, we replace the valve and the shut-off valve as a set, and we pull the city permit and pass the inspection. The service call on a weekday is $49, and it goes toward the work.

If you typed plumber near me because the shower goes thin after a minute or the pipes bang in the wall, the two numbers at the top belong to the Frisco office and the Plano office.

H2: What a Pressure Reducing Valve Does

A PRV, the water pressure regulator on your service line, takes the city's pressure on one side and gives the house a steady, lower pressure on the other. That's the whole job. The plumbing code caps the pressure inside a house at 80 PSI and requires a PRV wherever the city sends more, and here the city sends more. The people who make faucets, water heaters, dishwashers and washing machines rate them for 80 PSI too. Above that, every joint, every valve, every rubber seal and the water heater itself carries more pressure than it was built for. Below about 40 the house feels it the other way: a thin stream in the shower.

We set a new valve to about 70 PSI. It's the middle we like: well under 80, so the system isn't under stress, and well over 40, so nobody stands under a trickle. In parts of McKinney and Frisco the city's own line runs at 50 to 60 PSI, the city has come out and measured it, and there a new PRV gives you what the main gives it.

The valve is the homeowner's. The city's responsibility ends at the meter; what the pressure does inside the house is yours to check, and a gauge on an outside spigot once a year is the check that catches a valve before it fails.

H2: A Water Pressure Test First: the Gauge on the Spigot

A pressure gauge screws onto an outside spigot or onto the washing machine bib. With no water running in the house it shows the static pressure. Over 80 is a problem whether anything feels wrong or not, and at 100 nothing feels wrong at all. Then open a tap inside and watch the needle. A healthy system drops a little and holds. A clogged PRV holds the number while nothing runs and gives it up the moment something does.

[Рядом фото 228: 100 PSI on the gauge at an outside spigot, Frisco]

That is the pressure test, and it is where every pressure call of ours starts: the static pressure in the system, then the pressure under load, with water running. It's also the test we ask you to run once a year. On a house that reads fine, you've spent two minutes. On a house at 110, you've found the reason the toilet fill valves keep failing.

H2: Signs a Pressure Reducing Valve Is Failing

Three things, and each one is a different failure inside the same valve.

The first and the most common: the pressure drops when you use water. The gauge reads fine, even high, with everything closed. Open the shower, and for the first twenty or thirty seconds it runs like it should, because that's the water that was sitting in the pipes under pressure. Then it falls off. Open a second tap and it falls off more. That valve is clogged. Hard water has filled it with scale, and it passes pressure but not volume.

The second is the opposite: too much pressure. Water sprays out of every faucet, the toilet fill valves stop shutting off and the tank keeps running, and when you close a tap you hear the pipe bang in the wall. That bang is water hammer, and it's a PRV that has stopped holding the city back.

The third is plain: the valve leaks in its box. You see a wet spot in the flower bed, or the city sends a notice about water use, or you lift the lid once and the box is full of water. The part people don't think about until it happens: the house's own shut-off valve sits in that same box. A valve that has stood in water is rusted when you need it: the night a pipe goes and you run out to close the house, the handle comes off in your hand.

She flushed a toilet, and the pipes in the ceiling went bang, bang, bang. She opened the sink, and the stream slowly thinned out. That's two of the three signs in one bathroom. The house was about ten years old. She knew where the valve was supposed to be, in the flower bed, but the flower bed had only a box in it, and the box was under the dirt. We dug it out and found the original PRV and the secondary shut-off valve beside it, both corroded. Both came out. H2: Can a Bad PRV Cause Low Water Pressure?

Yes, and it's the failure we see most. Hard water scales the valve shut from the inside a little more every year. Nothing bursts, nothing leaks, the house just gets weaker: the shower first, then the second bathroom when somebody runs the kitchen tap, then the sprinklers. The gauge on the spigot can still read 60 or 70 with nothing running, which is why the valve gets blamed last. One Frisco house read 35 to 40 PSI on the gauge with the valve clogged, and 75 the same day with the new one in. In Frisco we have measured 30 on a valve that was packed solid inside, and about 40 in McKinney on one the homeowner had tried to adjust himself.

[Под текстом рядом: видео 82, 5 секунд без звука: Pressure 35 to 40 PSI: a broken PRV, one of the first signs, Frisco; фото 83: PRV replaced, pressure back to 75 PSI, Frisco]

Two other things read as low pressure here. The city itself: in a few areas of McKinney and Frisco the main carries 50 to 60 PSI, the city has measured it at the meter, and no valve makes more pressure than the city gives. And a valve nobody knows about: a second PRV in the same line, which is what the Plano story further down is about. The gauge, static and with a tap running, is what separates the three before anybody digs.

H2: How High Water Pressure Damages Plumbing

High pressure is quiet. At 100 PSI the shower feels great, and the house is wearing out early. The things that go first are the things with rubber in them: faucet cartridges, the fill valves in the toilets, the supply lines under the sinks, the washer hoses. Then the water heater, rated for 80 like everything else in the house. Slab leaks are on that list too: a copper line at 110 PSI for ten years has carried ten years of a load it wasn't built for.

On the Frisco page we tell where the newer houses leak first, at the valve box; on the McKinney page, what 108 PSI did to a shower valve. A plumber in Frisco and a plumber in McKinney keep finding the same thing: a house that felt fine and was over the line.

H2: How Long Does a Pressure Reducing Valve Last?

The usual figure is ten to fifteen years, and that's about right for a valve in a dry box with gravel under it. Here we see them fail from five. Two things shorten the life. The first is the box. A PRV is meant to sit in an accessible valve box with gravel on the bottom, so that rain and the sprinklers drain out instead of standing around the brass. When the builder buried it, or the ground moved and the box silted over, the valve sits in wet dirt and corrodes from the outside while the water scales it from the inside. The second is the water itself: hard, and it clogs the valve the way it clogs a shower head.

Most of the valves we replace are the builder's original. The house is ten years old or more, nobody has opened the box since the day it was set, and the first anybody hears of the PRV is the day it quits.

H2: Where the PRV Is Located in Frisco and Plano Homes

In most houses it's in the front flower bed, by the foundation, in a round or rectangular box with a lid, and the secondary shut-off valve for the house sits in the same box. Look for the lid first. If there's no lid in sight, the box has silted over, and finding it is part of our job. In many of the older Plano houses the box is gone under the dirt and the valve with it, and in many of those there was never a PRV at all, only the shut-off valve, as our plumber in Plano page shows.

[Рядом видео 262, 5 секунд без звука: The valve box where the PRV and the shut-off valve should be: opened, nothing but dirt, Prosper]

In the houses built in the last ten years the builders bring the main line into the garage, and the PRV and the shut-off valve stand on the wall there, behind a small access panel. That is a better place for it. No dirt, no water, you can check it in a minute and we replace it without a dig. It still fails. Some at five years, some at ten, some at fifteen; the garage protects the outside of the valve, not the inside. The automatic shut-off valves that go in beside it now, a Moen Flo on the same line, have their own guide on this site.

A second valve nobody knew about, Plano. A homeowner called about a wild vibration every time any tap opened. That points at the PRV, and the pressure was about 80, high enough. We found the buried box, put in a new PRV with a new ball valve beside it, rebuilt the box with gravel, and turned the water on. The vibration was still there. That was new. We walked the line out to the meter, opened a tap, and the meter itself was shaking. We called the city's Water Division and they came out fast. They took the meter out and we ran the line without it: still shaking. We took our new PRV out and ran a straight line: still shaking. The city put the meter back and left. We kept going along the line by hand, and 33 feet from the meter we found a second pressure reducing valve, buried, that nobody in the house or at the city knew was there. Two PRVs on one line, and nobody knew about the first one. We removed it, ran that spot straight, left our new valve where it was, set 70 PSI, and the house went quiet.

H2: Can a PRV Be Adjusted, or Does It Need Replacement?

A new PRV is adjusted, that's how it gets set: a bolt on top, turned until the gauge on the spigot reads 70. An old one is not adjusted, and it is not repaired either; there is nothing inside a scaled, rusted valve worth opening. That's a rule we made the hard way, years ago, and we don't break it. A valve that is ten years old, rusted on the outside and scaled on the inside, is held together by the fact that nobody has touched it. Turn the bolt and one of four things happens: it starts leaking at the bolt, something inside sticks, the pressure drops to nothing, or the pressure jumps. We've been called out to houses where the homeowner tried to adjust his own valve to get more pressure, and we've been asked to adjust old ones ourselves. The answer is the same. If it looks old and rusted, it's replaced.

The shut-off valve next to it goes at the same time. They were set the same day, they've stood in the same box in the same water, and if one has failed the other is not far behind. Digging the hole twice costs more than the second valve. So the PRV and the secondary shut-off valve are one job for us, always, and the gravel and the box come with it.

H2: How FPP Plumbing Replaces a PRV

The gauge goes on first, static and running, and the plumber tells you what it shows before the price. Then the box. If it's buried, we find it and dig it out; the hole goes deep enough for the new valves and a bed of gravel under them. The old PRV and the old shut-off valve come out together. The new ones go in, a PRV and a ball valve from the brands we trust, bought at Ferguson in McKinney, set onto the line. If the box is too shallow to cover the new valves, it gets an extension, so the lid closes over them and the dirt stays out. The pressure is set to 70, or lower if you ask, and we check it at the spigot. Then a tap runs inside while the plumber watches the gauge hold. The city permit is pulled before the dig and the inspector sees the work after; every city we work in asks for both on a PRV replacement.

120 PSI in Frisco. A house where the gauge at the old spigot read about 120. The PRV and the secondary shut-off valve were replaced, a drain valve went onto the line so the system can be emptied, and the outside spigot, which stood on a pipe out of the ground next to the box, was set again with a shut-off valve of its own, so it can be closed and drained for the winter. H2: The Expansion Tank: What a PRV Does to the Water Heater

A PRV is also a one way valve. Water comes in and nothing goes back out to the city. That makes the house a closed system, and a closed system has nowhere to put the water that expands every time the water heater heats a tank to 120 degrees. The pressure climbs with every cycle, and the rubber in the house takes the hit. The answer is an expansion tank on the water heater, a small tank with an air bladder that takes that extra water. If you have a PRV, and you almost certainly do, you have to have the tank. The expansion tank page covers the tank itself.

H2: Pressure Valve Questions We Get All the Time

What is normal water pressure for a house in Frisco or Plano? Between 40 and 80 PSI, and we set a new PRV to about 70. The city's line outside is higher than that almost everywhere here, 80 and up, often over 100, which is why the house needs the valve. Put a gauge on an outside spigot with nothing running: over 80 is too much, under 40 is why the shower is weak.

Where is the PRV located in my house? In the front flower bed by the foundation, in a box with a lid, next to the secondary shut-off valve. If there's no lid in sight, the box has silted over and the valve is under the dirt. In the newer houses it's on the garage wall behind an access panel.

How long does a PRV last in our hard water? Ten to fifteen years is the usual figure. With our hard water, and with the valve buried in wet dirt, we see them fail from five. A dry box with gravel under the valve gets you to the long end.

Can you adjust my old PRV instead of replacing it? No. An old rusted valve leaks, sticks, drops or jumps the moment the adjusting bolt turns, and we learned that years ago. A new valve is adjusted when it's set; an old one is replaced, together with the shut-off valve beside it.

Does PRV replacement need a city permit in Frisco or Plano? Yes, in both, and in every city we work in. We pull the permit before the dig and the city inspects the work after. The permit is in the price we give you.

Do I need an expansion tank if I have a PRV? Yes. The PRV closes the system, the water heater expands the water every cycle, and without a tank the pressure in the house climbs until the heater's relief valve drips. If you have a PRV and no expansion tank on the heater, that's the next job.
