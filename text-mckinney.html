Low Water Pressure in McKinney and the PRV in the Flower Bed

The PRV is the valve that decides what pressure your house sees, whatever the city main delivers. In most McKinney houses it sits in a plastic box in the flower bed by the foundation, with the second shut-off valve beside it, and every year the mulch buries both a little deeper. By the time somebody digs one up, the first touch is usually what finishes it: the stem starts to weep, or the handle breaks off the shut-off next to it.

A clogged PRV holds the house low. Low water pressure in a McKinney house looks like this: the shower is fine until a toilet fills, two open spigots can't run together, and the gauge on the hose bib reads 40. A PRV that stopped regulating lets the house run at whatever the main gives, and in parts of McKinney that is over 100 PSI. Nothing feels wrong at 100 PSI. The parts that pay for it are the ones that cost money: the water heater, the supply lines, every cartridge, the fill valve in every toilet, and in an older house the copper under the slab. Over 80 PSI the PRV gets adjusted or replaced, and we set it to about 70. A gauge on the hose bib once a year, two minutes, tells you where the house stands; we say that to every homeowner, and it's the same advice plumbers in McKinney should all be giving.
